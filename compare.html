Why Cortex comes out cheaper

Most third-party AI for Roblox is a reseller: it buys frontier models from OpenAI or Anthropic and charges you a marked-up rate on every token. That's why a game with a few hundred thousand NPC replies can quietly rack up a huge Robux bill. Cortex runs its own pooled model access instead of reselling per token — and that lets it price on a different, much cheaper basis. Two structural reasons:

  • You pay for output only. A reseller bills every token — including the system prompt, memory and chat history your NPC resends on every single call. Cortex bills the output only; the context you send is free, and repeated calls are served from cache for nothing.
  • Flat rate across models. On a reseller a frontier reply can cost 50–100× a cheap one. On Cortex every model bills at the same rate — 25 units per Robux (a unit = 1,000 output tokens) — because the pool absorbs the model-cost difference. Frontier quality, cheap-model price.

In practice: the 499 R$ package is 12,475 units = ~187,000 NPC lines. The same volume of frontier-quality replies on a per-token reseller runs into the hundreds of thousands of Robux — often 100×+ more — once you count the context billed on every call.

Why not just use Roblox's native API?

It's free and it's native — great for basic, low-stakes text. But it runs basic open-source models, so the writing is weaker and the range is narrower. The trade is simple: Roblox native gives you free-but-basic; Cortex gives you frontier-but-cheap. If your NPCs, quests or moderation need to actually feel smart, that gap shows up fast.

Why not just use OpenRouter, Claude or Ollama directly?

Because a Roblox game can't hand a credit card to OpenRouter — and your players pay you in Robux, not USD. Going direct means:

  • You need a credit card. OpenRouter, Claude and Ollama Cloud all bill in USD. Plenty of Roblox devs don't have one — Cortex takes Robux, so your AI is paid for in the same currency your game earns.
  • You hold the provider key, and one leak in client code drains your balance. With Cortex the key never leaves the server and is scoped to your game.
  • You're billed on every token — input, output, and the context you resend each call. Cortex bills output only; context and cached repeats are free.
  • A single key rate-limits at scale. Cortex pools access, so 100 concurrent players don't 429 you.

Going direct is fine if you have a card, a backend and low traffic. For a Robux-native Roblox game, Cortex closes the loop.

What you actually pay for — no fine print

The most common complaint about AI resellers is that you can't tell what you're buying. Here's the whole truth about Cortex in one place:

  • Output tokens only. The input — system prompt, memory, chat history you resend every call — is free.
  • Cached repeats are free. The same NPC line twice bills once.
  • One flat rate across every model, frontier included: 25 units per Robux (a unit = 1,000 output tokens). No per-model price jumps.
  • A pooled multi-provider backend — that's why it's cheaper and doesn't rate-limit like a single key. Models are frontier-class (GPT-5 / Claude class).
  • Beta, said plainly: ~3 seconds a call, no 24/7 SLA yet. You'll always know what tier you're on.
  • Usage in a clean web dashboard — not buried in bot messages or a made-up format.
